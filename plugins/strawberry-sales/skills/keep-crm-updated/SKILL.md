# Keep sales records and pipeline data current

This is a starting point for setting up a CRM workflow with a user. Adapt it to their system,
pipeline, and preferred level of review, then preserve the accepted process as their own skill.

## Context, setup, and planning

Try to understand:

- where customer data lives and how the user wants help keeping it current;
- which approved sources are useful, such as email threads, meetings, LinkedIn, existing notes, or
  prior CRM activity;
- what should create or update a record, log an activity, change a status or stage, create a
  follow-up, or be ignored;
- what “clean” means in their system, including duplicate handling, required fields, and when an
  uncertain match should be reviewed.

Existing CRM structure and connected context can help when available, but they are not
prerequisites. Offer to inspect that context, ask a few questions, or combine both. Prefer the CRM
app tool over navigating its website when one is available.

Before editing records, show a small representative batch with the source, target record, proposed
field changes, and reason. Let the user correct the mapping and review behavior. Record edits should
be made with their awareness rather than silently.

## Execution

1. Match records conservatively and deduplicate against existing customers, contacts, and prior
   activity. Surface uncertain matches instead of guessing.
2. Enrich or correct records from approved sources when useful. Preserve the evidence behind job,
   title, company, or status changes and flag inactive contacts, acquisitions, closures, or champion
   job changes for review.
3. When logging outreach, preserve the date, exact message, and relevant conversation context. After
   a reply or meeting, use the full thread, notes, or transcript when available; update the agreed
   status or stage and create the appropriate follow-up task or reminder.
4. For pipeline hygiene, surface records that may need attention, such as deals with no activity in
   7+ days, passed close dates, or missing key fields. Suggest actions rather than silently changing
   the pipeline.
5. Apply only the reviewed changes. Report any skipped, ambiguous, or conflicting records.

## Suggested outcome

Produce reviewable, deduplicated CRM updates with source traceability, accurate activity history,
and no silent stage or field changes.

## Suggested next steps

Once the mapping has worked reliably, save it as a user-owned skill. If the user wants ongoing
hygiene, offer a weekly Routine that follows the same approved mapping and review behavior.
