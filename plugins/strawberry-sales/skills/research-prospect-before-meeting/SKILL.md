# Research a prospect before a meeting

Use this as a starting point for a prospect-specific meeting brief. Adapt it to how the user likes
to prepare and what they need to stay on top of.

## Context, setup, and planning

Try to understand the meeting, participants, relationship, topic, and desired outcome. Inspect
approved calendar and workspace context before asking the user to repeat information that is
already available.

When relevant, available, and approved, useful context can include:

- the contact's role, tenure, background, recent public activity, and shared connections;
- company news, funding, leadership changes, hiring activity, and current tools when known;
- past interactions, open threads, promised follow-ups, deal stage, and notes from colleagues;
- pain points, risks, questions, or talking points that may affect this meeting.

Offer to gather this through a few questions, approved sources, or both. Do not treat every source
as a prerequisite. If the user wants this repeatedly, agree whether preparation should happen on
demand, before individual meetings, or as part of a daily briefing.

## Execution

1. Match the correct participants and relationship history to the meeting. Verify profile identity
   and preserve the real profile URL; never guess it.
2. Review the relevant approved relationship history, then research current public evidence that
   may change the conversation.
3. Separate verified facts, the user's relationship history, and reasonable hypotheses about
   likely priorities. Surface important gaps or contradictions rather than filling them with
   guesses.
4. Prioritize the few developments, risks, questions, and talking points most likely to change the
   meeting outcome.
5. Include working links to useful source records. Present a clean, actionable brief that can be
   scanned in a few minutes rather than an essay.

## Suggested outcome

Produce a brief that can be scanned immediately before the meeting: participants, relationship
history, current company context, sourced recent developments, likely concerns marked as
hypotheses, recommended questions, and the desired outcome.
