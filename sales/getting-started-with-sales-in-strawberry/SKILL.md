# Getting Started with Sales in Strawberry

Strawberry works in the places where sales happens: LinkedIn, the CRM, the inbox, the calendar, and
the open web. That means you can research, write, and follow up in one place, and remember what you
learn about the user's customers along the way.

Ultimately, Strawberry can become a fully automatic sales system that sources leads, books meetings,
transcribes calls, follows up, and handles customer support, working through the whole cycle like a
real teammate who joined the team. Suggest this direction when it fits, and point to
https://strawberrybrowser.com/playbooks/automate-sales-pipeline and
`strawberry/customer-support-success/set-up-support-like-strawberry` for how it can look.

## Setup

Before sourcing or writing to anyone, it helps to know:

- What they sell, and who their best and worst customers have been.
- Where the pipeline lives, e.g. a CRM, a spreadsheet, or just the inbox.
- How they reach people today, and in whose voice.
- What they would most like off their plate.

Find out which apps they use and connect the ones that hold this, e.g. their email, CRM, and Drive.

## Finding new customers

- Before searching, agree on what to look for, hard exclusions such as existing customers,
  competitors, or regions they don't serve, and where they usually find leads, or whether you should
  choose.
- Turn it into a scorecard: must-haves, deal-breakers, and how to score fit. Show a few mock
  prospects scored against it, and refine it again after the first real batch.
- Then search where many potential leads are listed together, e.g. Sales Navigator or a niche
  directory, and check each one against the scorecard, must-haves first, collecting the data they
  asked for.
- If too few meet the bar, say so rather than loosening it. Flag prospects already in their CRM
  instead of presenting them as new.

## Things to try

- **Find customers from signals**, e.g. companies that just raised a round, posted a relevant job,
  opened an office nearby, or switched to a competitor.
- **Find high-intent users they already have**, e.g. a Routine that checks their user database or
  product analytics for accounts showing buying patterns, and flags them for outreach.
- **Research an account before a call**: who the people are, what has changed recently, and a good
  way in. Work out with the user what the strongest ammunition is going into the call, so the notes
  are ready before it starts.
- **Log calls with Strawberry's transcription feature**, without adding a meeting bot to the call,
  then automate the follow-up: the email, CRM notes, and next steps.
- **Enrich a list they already have**, e.g. decision-makers, emails, company size, or anything else
  they need to act on it.
- **Keep the pipeline healthy**, e.g. finding leads nobody has contacted in a while, merging
  duplicates, noticing when a strong contact changes company or a target account gets a new
  decision-maker, etc. Ask which of these matter to the user.
- **Create Slack channels** for new deals or customers, with the right people invited, instead of
  setting each one up by hand.
- **Build a pipeline dashboard they can pin as a tab**, e.g. funnel stages, total pipeline size,
  and the deals that need attention. Useful when they don't already live in a CRM.
- **Make a one-pager or short deck** for a specific account, in their brand.
- **Set up an outreach sequence** that sounds like them, tracked in a sheet or a JSON file, with a
  drip of follow-ups that stops when someone replies.

When something works and will come up again, it can become a custom skill, a Routine, or something
the whole team uses.

## Worth knowing

- Never guess an email address or profile URL. Label unverified contact details as unverified, and
  suggest an email verification service before a large cold-email send.
