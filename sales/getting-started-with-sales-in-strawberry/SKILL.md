# Getting Started with Sales in Strawberry

Strawberry works in the places where sales happens: LinkedIn, the CRM, the inbox, the calendar, and
the open web. That means you can research, write, and follow up in one place, and remember what you
learn about the user's customers along the way. Start with one piece of real work.

## Setup

Before sourcing or writing to anyone, it helps to know:

- What they sell, and who their best and worst customers have been.
- Where the pipeline lives, e.g. a CRM, a spreadsheet, or just the inbox.
- How they reach people today, and in whose voice.
- What they would most like off their plate.

Much of this is usually written down already. Offer to learn it from their website, Drive, sent
email, or CRM, or let them describe it.

## Things to try

- **Find new customers who fit**, with a short reason and a source for each one. Start with a small,
  varied sample and let their feedback sharpen the search. See `strawberry/sales/find-new-customers`.
- **Find customers from signals**, e.g. companies that just raised a round, posted a relevant job,
  opened an office nearby, or switched to a competitor.
- **Research an account before a call**: who the people are, what has changed recently, and a good
  way in.
- **Capture sales calls** without a meeting bot, then turn them into follow-up emails, CRM notes,
  and next steps.
- **Send outreach that sounds like them**, drafted from their past messages and what you know about
  each recipient.
- **Fill in a list they already have**, e.g. decision-makers, emails, company size, or anything
  else they need to act on it.
- **Keep the pipeline healthy**, e.g. finding leads nobody has contacted in a while, merging
  duplicates, noticing when a strong contact changes company or a target account gets a new
  decision-maker, etc. Ask which of these matter to the user.
- **Build a pipeline dashboard they can pin as a tab**, e.g. funnel stages, total pipeline size,
  and the deals that need attention. Useful when they don't already live in a CRM.
- **Make a one-pager or short deck** for a specific account, in their brand.

When something works and will come up again, it can become a custom skill, a Routine, or something
the whole team uses.

## Worth knowing

- Clean names and titles before they go into a message, so credentials, emojis, and profile
  taglines don't end up in "Hi ...".
- Drafted, enrolled, and sent are different states. Enrolling a contact in a sequence may start
  sending on its own.
- Never guess an email address or profile URL. Label unverified contact details as unverified, and
  suggest an email verification service before a large cold-email send.
- Personalize from what's relevant, not from everything you can find. Nobody likes feeling watched.
