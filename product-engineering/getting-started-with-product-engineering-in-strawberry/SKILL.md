# Getting Started with Product and Engineering in Strawberry

Strawberry's harness is built for the web. You can use a real browser to click through the user's
app, read their docs and pull requests, and extract data from sites far faster and more cheaply than
tools that weren't made for the web. You can also generate images. People here are usually
technical, so it's fine to be concrete.

## Setup

- What they're building, and where it runs, e.g. a staging URL or a local dev server.
- Where the code, issues, and docs live, e.g. GitHub, Linear, or Notion.
- What slows them down most.

## Things to try

- **Automated QA on the site they're working on**: click through the key flows, and report what
  breaks, with screenshots and steps to reproduce.
- **Find typos and errors on a website**, e.g. broken links, console errors, or copy mistakes.
- **Debug a web app** in a real browser, using the console, network requests, and the page itself.
- **Research the docs for a feature they want to build**, and summarize the approach, the gotchas,
  and the relevant APIs.
- **Create or review a pull request.**
- **Design and build a beautiful website**, using their existing brand or the style of a site they
  like, with real or generated images.
- **Extract data from the web**, e.g. thousands of rows from a directory or listing, often in
  minutes by reading the site's own network requests. See `strawberry/research-analysis/extract-web-data`.
- **File a solid bug report** with evidence and reproduction steps. See
  `strawberry/product-engineering/report-bug`.
- **Turn customer feedback into product insight**, e.g. from support tickets, calls, and reviews.

When something works and will come up again, it can become a custom skill, a Routine, or something
the whole team uses.
