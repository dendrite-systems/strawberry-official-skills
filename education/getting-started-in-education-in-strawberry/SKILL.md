# Getting Started in Education in Strawberry

Help the user prepare one real piece of teaching, then show them the workflow behind it. Preparation
time is the scarce resource, and most of it goes on finding current material and pitching it at the
right level rather than on the teaching itself.

Work from the user's curriculum, specification, or set texts when they have them. A generic
explanation of a topic is easy to find; one that matches what their students will actually be
assessed on is not.

## Pitch the explanation at the right level

Ask who the students are and what they already know. The same topic needs a different explanation
for a first-year undergraduate, a GCSE class, and a professional cohort, and the usual failure is
material that is accurate but pitched past the room.

Offer several explanations of the same idea — an analogy, a worked example, a common misconception
to correct — rather than one canonical version. Teachers know which will land with their group.

## Prepare material from current sources

Research current, credible material and keep the link and date beside anything the user will present
as fact. Use `strawberry/research-analysis/map-a-market` when the user is teaching into a field they
do not know well and needs the main positions, sources, and current debates before building a
course.

Use `strawberry/research-analysis/research-a-company` for an organization, institution, or employer
the user needs to understand — for a case study, a placement, or a partnership.

Never invent a source, statistic, or quotation. Material presented to a class carries the teacher's
authority behind it.

## Turn it into something teachable

Use `strawberry/research-analysis/create-beautiful-slide-deck` when the result should be presented.
Keep the structure driven by what students need to understand in sequence, not by what looks
complete on a slide.

## Give feedback without doing the work for them

When helping with marking or feedback, work from the user's rubric and their own judgment. Draft
comments they can edit, identify patterns across a set of work, and flag where a criterion is being
applied inconsistently. Do not assign final grades on the user's behalf.

## Stay on top of the term

Use `strawberry/operations/close-open-loops` to catch commitments, unanswered messages, and
follow-ups owed to students and colleagues, and `strawberry/operations/prepare-a-status-update` when
faculty, parents, or a department need a concise update.

After a workflow proves useful, preserve the accepted level, structure, sources, and feedback style
as a custom skill so the next module starts there. Suggest a Routine only for something genuinely
recurring, such as watching a syllabus area or publication for material worth teaching.
