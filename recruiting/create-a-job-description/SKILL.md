# Create a Job Description

Define the job clearly enough for the hiring team and candidates to make good decisions. The
internal role profile and external job description may share facts, but they serve different
audiences and should not be collapsed into one document by default.

## 1. Establish the role decision

Clarify whether the user needs an internal role profile, an external job description, a revision,
or both. Understand the business need, decisions due, hiring timeline, reporting line, intended
level, location and working pattern, and who must approve the result.

Use approved context already available in workforce plans, role-design notes, team documents,
existing JDs, scorecards, leveling or compensation frameworks, and relevant policy or legal
guidance. Ask only about gaps or conflicts that materially change the role.

When revising an existing role, determine what has actually changed: the business need, outcomes,
scope, level, market, hiring evidence, policy, or merely the wording. Do not carry obsolete criteria
forward because they appear in the old document.

## 2. Define the internal role profile

Build the role around the outcomes expected in the first 6–12 months and the scope needed to own
them. Make clear:

- the business rationale, reporting line, responsibilities, decision rights, and interfaces;
- level, location, working pattern, compensation constraints, and eligibility requirements;
- job-related must-haves, useful adjacent backgrounds, and genuine dealbreakers;
- how the hiring team could observe each important capability; and
- assumptions, risks, policy checks, and unresolved decisions.

Challenge requirement inflation, vague traits, copied credentials, contradictory constraints, and
criteria that do not connect to the work. Do not introduce protected or irrelevant personal
characteristics. If the role is not defined well enough for an honest external description, keep
the unresolved decision visible rather than smoothing it over.

## 3. Write the candidate-facing description

Translate the accepted internal profile into an honest external job description. Explain the role
story, outcomes, responsibilities, team, scope, qualifications, location and working pattern,
compensation where required or approved, process, and accessibility or accommodation information
when supplied.

Use plain language a credible candidate can evaluate. Separate essential requirements from useful
experience, remove internal shorthand, and avoid claims about culture, progression, flexibility,
compensation, or impact that the approved sources do not support.

If internal and external versions are requested, deliver them separately and show how the
candidate-facing wording maps back to the accepted outcomes and evidence bar.

## 4. Review the draft

Provide a concise change summary when revising an existing document. Mark assumptions, policy or
legal checks, compensation decisions, and unresolved questions visibly. Let the hiring team correct
the outcomes, level, evidence bar, tone, and candidate promise before the document moves anywhere.

A qualified human approves the role definition, requirements, level, compensation, and wording.
The skill prepares decision support and drafts; it does not decide who should be hired or whether a
criterion is legally compliant.

## 5. Continue within the current permission

Follow Strawberry's active scoped permission for the source systems, job, account, destination,
and action. Creating a draft, sharing it internally, changing an ATS record, publishing to a careers
page or job board, and sending it to candidates are separate actions. Draft or ask when permission
is insufficient, stop when identity, scope, impact, or sensitive-data handling changes, and verify
completed external actions.

After approval, use `strawberry/recruiting/map-a-talent-market` when feasibility or search strategy
needs testing, `strawberry/recruiting/source-candidates` when the role is ready for a shortlist, and
`strawberry/recruiting/prepare-candidate-interviews` to turn the accepted criteria into a structured
interview plan.

Offer to save the accepted role-profile or JD structure as a custom or team skill when the team
will reuse it. Add a Routine only for a stable review or publication-preparation process with a real
trigger, destination, approval behavior, and stop conditions—not for role-design judgment.
