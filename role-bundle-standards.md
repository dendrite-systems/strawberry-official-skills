# Role bundle standards

Role bundles are Getting Started entry points plus focused skills owned by the right collection.
They share a recognizable journey without forcing every skill into the same template:
**understand → gather or plan → calibrate when useful → execute → validate → take permitted action
→ deliver, learn, and reuse**.

## Practical standards

- **Paint the system before the first task:** A Getting Started guide first gives the user a brief,
  credible picture of how the role's work can connect in Strawberry. Only then should it diagnose
  the immediate priority or recommend a first win. Focused skills can begin directly with the task.
- **Use a model, not a forced story:** Give the role one memorable system, loop, or set of connected
  lanes, then use distinct sections for the real workflows. A hero workflow may receive extra
  emphasis, but it should not imply that every user follows one sequence or passes through one
  anchor. Do not invent a persona or case-study narrative to hold the bundle together.
- **Route or add:** Route when an existing skill already owns the inputs, specialist judgment,
  review points, and result. Add only when the work itself differs, not just the audience. A role
  guide explains the handoff; it does not copy the focused workflow.
  - **Worked boundary—meeting preparation and account research:** Meeting preparation owns the
    upcoming conversation, time remaining, relationship context, desired outcome, and concise
    brief. Account research owns deeper company and stakeholder investigation when those gaps could
    change the conversation. Preparation hands up to research when depth is needed; research hands
    down to preparation when the next job is turning evidence into a meeting plan. Keep the two
    owners and their outputs distinct instead of merging or duplicating them.
- **Merchandise across roles deliberately:** Add a secondary-role use case when that role performs
  the same recurring job, the focused skill's judgment transfers without a role-specific fork, and
  discovery would materially improve. Do not add every plausible audience or use cross-role reach
  as a quota. The selected functional bundle remains the main discovery surface for organization
  overlays such as Agency; add overlay-specific presentations only when client separation,
  delivery, or another overlay concern changes how the shared owner should be found.
- **Use one discovery-weight scale:** Weights express how central a presentation is for the selected
  role, not the global quality of a skill. Use `120–124` for that role's Getting Started entry,
  `112–116` for a journey head or flagship, `100–110` for core role work, `84–98` for useful
  adjacent work, and `82` or below for peripheral or fallback routes. A focused skill does not use
  the Getting Started band merely because a role lacks a guide. Secondary-role presentations are
  normally lower than the skill's primary-role presentation. Use `good-first-skill` only when the
  package can produce a bounded useful result without a large setup; the tag is not a substitute
  for the weight ladder.
- **Evidence:** Separate sourced facts, observations, inferences, assumptions, missing evidence,
  and product judgment. Be honest when research is thin. Consequential claims remain traceable to
  their sources.
- **First result:** Start with a bounded piece of real work from context the user has approved.
  Connect another app only when it materially improves the result, and calibrate with a small,
  varied sample only when correction before scale is valuable. Fast first value follows the
  role-level picture; it does not replace it.
- **Use judgment before quotas:** Reserve fixed counts, cadences, sequences, and required steps for
  work whose reliability, safety, or cost genuinely depends on them. Otherwise describe the
  intended quality—such as a small prioritized set—and adapt to the user's existing rhythm rather
  than inventing one.
- **Execute and validate:** A focused skill does the work, checks the result, and returns a useful,
  reviewable artifact. Keep only task-specific judgment, source choices, calibration, outputs, and
  consequential boundaries; cut generic procedural padding.
- **Permissions:** Follow Strawberry's active scoped permission for the action, account,
  destination, and conditions. Draft or ask when permission is insufficient. Stop when identity,
  scope, impact, or sensitive-data handling changes. Verify completed external actions.
- **Editorial:** Use the established `Getting Started with <role> in Strawberry` name unless natural
  grammar calls for a small variation. Articles open like a human explanation, make one clear
  product argument, show the few Strawberry differences that matter, and use an early entry card.
  Keep the opening lean enough to scan even when the body is rich: near the top, a reader should be
  able to identify the work, why Strawberry fits, the larger role system, and a useful way to begin.
  This is a page-hierarchy test, not a fixed paragraph or word-count rule. A Getting Started article
  is the role's operating-system map; focused articles teach the individual workflows. Use a linear
  progression only when the work really progresses that way, and use tables, lists, or numbered
  sequences only when they make the page easier to understand. Shared-skill cards appear only at
  genuine branches—not as an inventory or prompt library. Run a sentence-economy pass after the
  structure is sound: tighten headings, table labels, transitions, and coaching language without
  removing the examples or connective reasoning that make the page useful.
- **Make the product difference concrete:** A reader should not leave wondering why the workflow
  belongs in Strawberry rather than an ordinary chat. Near the opening, establish the relevant
  connected working surface; later, repeat a product mechanism only where it changes the workflow.
  Useful mechanisms include visible browser navigation, logged-in tools, cross-app context,
  source inspection, memory of accepted corrections, user takeover, scoped actions, and verified
  completion. Connect each mechanism to the role constraint and user outcome; do not turn the
  article into a feature roll-call or repeat the whole product argument in every section.
- **Public article routes:** A skill card opens the focused article when one is published. Keep an
  unlinked workflow card in the operating-system map when its article is not ready; do not send the
  reader from an article to a raw-only skill page. For now, a focused article's own card may jump to
  the raw skill embedded on that page. Treat the embedded raw file as a deferred product decision,
  not the permanent article CTA. Do not assume the Strawberry app is installed or auto-run work
  from a public page without an explicit launch-and-fallback design.
- **Organization context versus functional ownership:** Agency is an organization overlay: it adds
  client separation, delivery, reporting, and approval context while routing specialist work to the
  selected function. Consulting owns distinct advisory deliverables such as proposals, engagement
  briefs, and recommendations, so it can be a functional bundle while still reusing shared Sales,
  Research, Operations, and Agency context. Decide from the result a collection owns rather than
  treating every client-service label as either an overlay or a function.
- **Reuse:** Preserve accepted sources, criteria, format, ownership, and review rules after a good
  run. Share an artifact, method, companion, or Routine only when its audience, access, owner,
  trigger, destination, and stop conditions are clear.

## Finished-bundle check

A fresh user can recognize the entry point, reach the canonical owner, get a source-linked first
result, understand the next decision, and take only permitted actions. The bundle passes package,
article, route, discovery, importer, and representative behavioral checks; superseded ids and
duplicate workflows are resolved; evidence gaps and live validation needs are recorded outside the
production package. A fresh reader should also be able to describe the larger role system and see
how the first workflow could connect to later work.
